## Overview

This repository contains the full Python source code for the paper *Diversification Under Stress: Empirical Evidence of Correlation Breakdown Across Sectors and International Markets — A Stress-Conditional Analysis with the Trachsler Resilience Score*.

The paper investigates whether portfolio diversification across U.S. sectors and international equity markets retains its risk-reducing properties under stress. Using daily return data from 1999 to 2025 spanning ten sector ETFs and eight international equity indices, we document a systematic correlation breakdown during crisis episodes and introduce the **Trachsler Resilience Score** as a formal criterion for constructing stress-robust portfolios.

## Data

Data are retrieved from Yahoo Finance using the `yfinance` Python library. To reproduce the data files, run the data download section at the top of `analysis.py`. The following ETFs are used:

**Sector panel (1999–2025):** XLK, XLF, XLV, XLE, XLI, XLY, XLP, XLU, XLB, IYR

**International panel (2003–2025):** SPY, IEV, EWJ, EEM, EWU, EWG, EWZ, EPP

All figures are saved as high-resolution PNG files in the `plots/` directory.

## Key Results

- Correlation breakdown is systematic: in **71.2%** of 59 independently identified stress events, mean pairwise correlations rose above the pre-event baseline (mean Δρ = 0.094, p < 0.0001)
- The **nature** of a shock matters more than its magnitude: the Trump Tariff Shock of 2025 produced the highest Δρ in the entire dataset (+0.379), exceeding the GFC and COVID crash
- The **Trachsler Resilience Score** identifies sector combinations that remain relatively independent under stress
- Out-of-sample validation: the Resilience Portfolio reduces maximum drawdown in **83%** of independent stress events (p = 0.003)
